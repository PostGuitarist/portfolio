## Summary

Tethr helps you cut your screen time with help from friends you trust. It has no ads, no analytics, and no tracking, and we don't run servers that receive your data. Tethr has no account of its own: it uses your iCloud account, and everything it stores is kept by Apple in iCloud.

Your friends see only what you've approved them to see. You approve every friend who watches you, choose what each one hears about, and can remove anyone at any time. The sections below list exactly what leaves your iPhone and who can see it.

## What stays on your iPhone

- **The apps you pick.** You choose apps and categories in Apple's Screen Time picker. Apple gives Tethr opaque tokens for them, not names, so Tethr can block those apps without learning which ones they are.
- **Your rules.** Your daily limit, the apps that count, and your alert settings are stored on your iPhone, shared with Tethr's extensions on the same device.
- **Your usage.** Apple's Screen Time keeps track of how long you use your apps. By default, Tethr's usage cards (today's ring, where your time went, and the week chart) are drawn by a Screen Time report extension that Apple runs in a sandbox and that can't send anything off your iPhone.
- **Blocking.** When you pass your limit, Tethr blocks the apps you picked using Apple's Screen Time. This happens on your iPhone.
- **Your own reminders.** Notifications for nearing your limit, or for having a good day, are scheduled and delivered on your iPhone. A good day is your own celebration, and your friends aren't told.

## Screen Time data access

On iOS 26.4 and later, Apple lets you give one app access to your Screen Time data. Tethr asks for it, and works without it.

If you give Tethr data access, it can read your app usage itself. Two features use that:

- **The friend view.** Friends who watch you can see your usage against your limit for today and this week.
- **Detailed alerts.** If you choose Detailed for a friend, alerts sent to that friend include the name of the app that took most of your time and how long you spent in it (for example, "Most of it was Instagram, 1h 05m").

Without data access, no usage data leaves your iPhone, and your friends only receive the alerts described below. You can turn data access off at any time in the iPhone Settings app, under Screen Time.

## What your friends receive

A friend who watches you is someone you've approved. Each friend gets their own private copy of what you share with them, which only they can read. Depending on your settings, it includes:

- **Your profile.** The name you chose in Tethr and your daily limit.
- **Alerts.** When you pass your limit, when you open a blocked app, and when you ask for a nudge. You can switch the first two off per friend, or pause a friend for the day, and a nudge only goes to the friends you pick. An alert includes the time and the kind of event. It also includes the custom message you wrote for that friend, if you have Tethr Plus and set one, and app names and minutes for Detailed alerts, as above.
- **Reactions and replies.** Your friends can react to or reply to your alerts, and you can do the same for theirs. These are shared between the two of you.
- **Your day.** Your usage against your limit, only if you've given Tethr Screen Time data access, as described above.

Your friends get these as push notifications and in the app. A notification that has already been delivered stays on their iPhone until they clear it.

## How Tethr stores data in iCloud

Tethr uses Apple's CloudKit, under your iCloud account, for friends and alerts. We can't read your private iCloud data.

- **Your private iCloud database.** Your profile, your friend code, your settings for each friend, and the alerts, reactions, and replies for each friend are stored in your own private iCloud database. What you share with each friend is kept in a separate part of it that's shared with that one friend through iCloud sharing, and with no one else.
- **Tethr's public database.** To let friends find each other, a few records are kept in Tethr's public iCloud database: your friend code with your display name and an identifier for your iCloud account that Apple assigns to Tethr, and any friend requests you send or receive (both names, the direction of the friendship, and a link to the shared data). Anyone using Tethr who knows the code or identifier can read these records, which is how a friend request reaches the other person. Linked data opens only for the friend it's shared with. Requests are deleted once they're answered, or after 14 days.
- **Push notifications.** Apple delivers alerts to your friends through its push notification service. The readable text of each alert ("Alex hit their daily limit on Tethr.") is put together on your friend's iPhone.

Apple's handling of iCloud data is covered by [Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

## Inviting friends

You can invite a friend with a link, a friend code, or a QR code. Each one only sends a request: nothing is shared until the other person approves it.

Tethr doesn't read your contacts. If you send an invite to a contact, iOS opens Messages with the link filled in, and you choose whether to send it.

Invite links open Tethr's website, which only tells your iPhone to open the link in Tethr or the App Store. The website doesn't use cookies or analytics.

## Purchases

Tethr Plus is an optional subscription sold through the App Store. Apple handles payment, and we never see your payment details. Tethr asks the App Store which subscriptions are active on your Apple Account so it can unlock Plus features.

## Permissions

Tethr asks for these only when you use the feature that needs them. You can change any of them later in the iPhone Settings app.

- **Screen Time.** Used to block the apps you pick at your limit and to show your usage. Data access is a separate choice, described above.
- **Notifications.** Used for alerts and reactions from your friends, and for your own reminders.
- **iCloud.** Friends need an iCloud account. Without one, Tethr still sets limits and blocks apps on your iPhone, but you can't add friends.

## Children

Tethr isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from anyone. We hold no data about any user: everything Tethr stores is kept by Apple in iCloud.

## Deleting your data

- **Remove a friend.** Removing a friend, or turning off the side of a friendship where they watch you, deletes what you shared with them and ends their access to it.
- **Delete your account.** Settings → Delete my account deletes everything you've shared with friends, your profile and settings in iCloud, your friend code, and your friend requests. It also removes your rules and settings from your iPhone.
- **Export your data.** Settings → Export my data saves a copy of your profile, friends, and activity.

Deleting the app removes the data on your iPhone, but not what's stored in iCloud. To remove that too, use **Delete my account** first. Deleting your data doesn't cancel a Tethr Plus subscription. To cancel, use your Apple Account's subscription settings.
